# Who calls the patient today: the municipal follow-up burden

Workstream 3 of the Phase 0 research brief, re-scoped on 3 August 2026.

Consulted 3 August 2026. Author: Felipe Carvajal Brown. Archived copies of every PDF cited here are
in `docs/research/sources/`.

## Why this workstream was re-scoped

Workstream 2 left an open question: whether anything actually happens between PSCV controls. I found
no routine between-controls contact in national guidance and recorded it as unverified rather than
concluding that nothing happens.

Felipe corrected the framing: patients **are** called, by municipal health staff, and this happens in
every comuna. PreventIA's claim is not that nobody is watching. It is that somebody is watching, at
the cost of a person's working day, in a system where those people are overloaded — and that the
product substitutes for that call rather than for the control.

That is a different argument and a better one, because it has a cost attached and a named person
carrying it. This document establishes what the calling work actually is, who does it, at what scale,
and — the part that cuts against us — what the one national attempt to automate it actually achieved.

The original workstream 3 (adherence measurement and remote follow-up evidence) runs next.

## 1. The calling work is formally mandated, not informal

The PSCV Orientación Técnica defines a **sistema de rescate de inasistentes** with binding
definitions, quoted verbatim:

> **Inasistencias:** cualquier paciente que haya faltado a su control programado sin re-agendamiento.
>
> **Pasivos:** Pacientes inasistentes a sus controles por más de 11 meses y 29 días, y que no se ha
> realizado ninguna actividad de rescate. No pueden ser egresados automáticamente del programa.
>
> **Abandono:** Aquellos usuarios que han permanecido inasistentes al programa por más de 11 meses 29
> días, y que se han efectuado, al menos, 3 acciones de rescate en dicho periodo.
>
> **Rescatados:** Usuarios que vuelven al programa después de haber estado en abandono sin rescate o
> pasivos.

Three consequences worth being precise about, because they define the work PreventIA would displace:

1. **A patient cannot be written off without being chased.** Discharge as *abandono* requires at
   least **3 documented rescate actions**. A centre that does not chase gets *pasivos* instead, and
   those "no pueden ser egresados automáticamente del programa" — the patient stays on the books and
   the obligation stays open.
2. **The window is nearly a year.** 11 months 29 days. The obligation to chase persists across it.
3. **It is a counted statistic.** These categories feed the REM returns, so rescate is measured
   administratively, not left to goodwill.

The OT also names urgency teams as a rescate route: emergency staff are described as well placed for
"el rescate de pacientes inasistente a los controles". Being chased after turning up at urgencias is
the system's fallback, which is the failure mode PreventIA claims to pre-empt.

**What the OT does not specify is the modality.** It says "acciones de rescate" without defining
whether that is a call, a visit or a letter. That definition lives in local protocols.

## 2. What a rescate action consists of

Local CESFAM protocols describe the sequence: weekly review of the files of patients who missed
controls, verification of non-attendance, **telephone contact**, and, where telephone contact fails,
a **home visit** by a staff member to the registered address. Effective contact is defined as direct
contact with the person, to which they respond, by telephone call, home visit, certified letter or
email.

**Sourcing caveat, stated plainly.** The protocol documents I could reach are hosted on Studocu and
Scribd — student and user-upload platforms — not on municipal or Servicio de Salud domains. I am
treating them as **indicative of common practice, not authoritative**, and no number from them
appears in this document. The escalation sequence they describe (phone first, home visit on failure)
is consistent across the ones consulted and consistent with the OT's framing, which is why it is
reported at all.

**Unverified:** the actual modality mix and the staff time a rescate action consumes. What would
verify it: one municipal or Servicio de Salud *protocolo de rescate* published on an official domain,
or a CESFAM team asked directly. **This is the single most valuable thing the clinical teammate could
supply for the pitch**, because it converts "we save staff time" from an assertion into a number.

## 3. Who carries it, and at what scale

### 3.1 It is municipal, overwhelmingly

From the DIPRES evaluation of the Fondo de Farmacia programme, which runs in the same APS
establishments as the PSCV: of the **2.027 establishments** in the country running the programme
(2018 data), **90,7% are of municipal dependency**, 8,7% belong to Servicios de Salud, and 0,6% to
NGOs and other non-profit institutions. In 2017 the equivalent figure by establishment activity was
86,3% municipal.

So "funcionarios de salud municipal, en todas las comunas" is accurate: nine in ten of the
establishments doing this work answer to a municipality, not to a Servicio de Salud.

### 3.2 The population being chased

From the same evaluation:

| Year | PSCV population under control |
|---|---|
| 2014 | 2.090.174 |
| 2015 | 2.163.914 |
| 2016 | 2.241.715 |
| 2017 | **2.301.144** |

2.301.144 people in 2017, described in the report as **13% of the national population**, growing
10,1% across the four years.

**And they are polymedicated, which the report quantifies.** Summing the populations under control by
individual pathology gives 3.799.639, against 2.301.144 actual people. The report's own conclusion:

> el 65% de la población bajo control en el PSCV en 2017 es diagnosticada con más de un problema de
> salud

**This substantiates PRD section 4 directly.** "Adultos mayores polimedicados" is not a niche the
project invented to make a demo interesting; multimorbidity is the majority case in the programme,
at 65%. It is the best single number in this whole workstream for the pitch, and it is official.

### 3.3 The arithmetic nobody has done for us

2,3 million people under control, seen every 3 to 6 months depending on cardiovascular risk
(workstream 2), across roughly 2.000 establishments that are 90% municipal, with an obligation to
attempt at least 3 rescate actions before anyone can be discharged for non-attendance.

I am deliberately **not** multiplying these into a headline "X million calls per year" figure. The
inputs come from different years and different denominators, non-attendance rates vary, and a
fabricated-looking number is exactly what a management-criteria judge will pick apart. The
components above are each individually sourced, and that is what should go on the slide.

## 4. The finding that cuts against us, and it is the important one

Chile has already tried automating this contact at national scale, and the government's own
evaluation found it barely worked.

### 4.1 What was tried

The **Fondo de Farmacia (FOFAR)** programme includes an appointment-management and messaging
component: reminding patients of the days they are called to collect their medication, "reforzando la
adherencia al tratamiento mediante la atención farmacéutica y la mensajería a los teléfonos celulares
o llamadas telefónicas a teléfonos fijos o celulares (sistema de gestión de citas)".

Text messages and phone calls, to reinforce adherence, at national scale.

### 4.2 What it achieved

DIPRES evaluation, Cuadro 20, covering **4.481.282 PSCV appointments** across 2016 and 2017:

| Year | Appointments reminded | Missed | % | Appointments not reminded | Missed | % |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 1.022.429 | 113.396 | **11,1%** | 1.037.755 | 126.720 | **12,2%** |
| 2017 | 1.318.447 | 157.658 | **12,0%** | 1.102.651 | 158.513 | **14,4%** |
| Total | 2.340.876 | 271.054 | **11,6%** | 2.140.406 | 285.233 | **13,3%** |

The panel's own conclusion, verbatim:

> se puede concluir que el recordatorio de citas tiene un efecto marginal respecto de la inasistencia
> a los controles, pues no se observa una diferencia importante en la inasistencia de los pacientes
> que recibieron recordatorio respecto de aquellos que no lo recibieron

and:

> el panel considera que el programa tiene una contribución marginal al logro de la adherencia a
> tratamientos

**A national programme, 4,5 million appointments, roughly 1,7 percentage points of difference, and
the evaluating panel calling the contribution marginal.**

### 4.3 The methodological caveat, which runs in our favour and must still be stated

This is not a randomised comparison and the report does not claim it is. The panel states its
assumption explicitly — "suponiendo que el resto de los factores que inciden en la asistencia a las
citas médicas son similares" — and that assumption is doubtful. The "not reminded" group failed to be
reminded **because their contact data was wrong or missing**, per the table's own footnote. Patients
with bad contact data are systematically different from patients with good contact data: more likely
to have moved, to be more marginalised, to be harder to reach in every other way.

So the true effect of a reminder could be larger than 1,7 points, or smaller. What the comparison
cannot support is a confident claim in either direction. Anyone citing this — including us — should
cite it as **the Chilean state's own evaluation finding a marginal effect, on a confounded
comparison**, and not as a clean null.

### 4.4 Coverage was, and may still be, small

The appointment-management system ran in **23 of 29 Servicios de Salud** but only **14,4% of APS
establishments** — 291 of 2.027 — "principalmente aquellos de mayor tamaño ubicados en centros
urbanos. En contraste, en postas rurales esa condición no es habitual."

Nearly six in seven APS establishments had no automated appointment contact at all. The evaluation
panel flagged this as inadequate.

**Currency caveat:** this is 2018 data about 2016-2017 operations. Coverage has very likely changed.
**Unverified.** What would verify it: a later DIPRES or MINSAL evaluation of FOFAR, or the current
programme's own reporting.

## 5. What this means for PreventIA

The honest reading, and it needs to be in the pitch rather than discovered by a judge.

**The precedent is a caution, not a validation.** The nearest Chilean thing to what we are building
is an SMS and phone reminder system, and the state's own evaluation called its contribution to
adherence marginal. If we claim "contacting patients improves adherence", the counter-evidence is
Chilean, national, and public.

**The distinction that survives is mechanism, not modality.** FOFAR sent a reminder: a one-way
message about an appointment date. PreventIA proposes a two-way conversation that extracts symptom
and adherence facts and escalates a ranked case to a clinician. Those are different interventions
that happen to share a telephone. Whether the second works is an open question; the failure of the
first does not settle it, and the success of the first would not have settled it either.

**Stated the way it should be said out loud:** reminders have been tried here at national scale and
the effect was marginal, which is a reason to build something that is not a reminder.

**The burden argument is the stronger one and does not depend on the adherence claim at all.** Even
if PreventIA changed no clinical outcome whatsoever, a system that conducts the rescate contact and
hands a municipal team a ranked list has displaced work those teams are formally obliged to do,
across 2.000 establishments that are 90% municipal, for a population of 2,3 million of whom 65% are
multimorbid. That argument stands on the mandate in section 1 and the scale in section 3, and it is
the one to lead with in front of a management-criteria judge.

**Two things this changes in the PRD**, carried into `docs/research/README.md` as proposals rather
than edits:

- PRD section 1's "En el intervalo no hay nadie mirando" is **not accurate** and should be replaced
  with the mandate-and-burden framing.
- PRD section 10's post-Lab success criterion needs to survive contact with section 4 above. That is
  the subject of the next workstream and is not resolved here.
