# Chilean primary-care reality: cardiovascular follow-up in the public system

Workstream 2 of the Phase 0 research brief (`docs/research/PROMPTforANGEL.md`).

Consulted 3 August 2026. Author: Felipe Carvajal Brown. Archived copies of every PDF cited here are
in `docs/research/sources/`.

## Headline: three numbers in PRD section 1 do not survive contact with the source

The brief asked for provenance on the 2.4 million / 400+ days / 500+ days figures because they will
be said in front of judges from government and health management. They needed checking, and two of
the three do not hold against the most recent official data.

| PRD section 1 claim | Most recent official figure | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "alrededor de 2,4 millones de personas esperan en listas del sistema público" | 2.088.245 people waiting for a new specialty consultation, plus 398.496 waiting for surgery, at 31 March 2026 | **Not attributable as stated.** See 1.2. |
| "El promedio de espera supera los 400 días para una consulta de especialidad" | National **mean** 329 days; median 236 days | **Contradicted.** |
| "y los 500 días para cirugía" | National **mean** 383 days; median 259 days | **Contradicted.** |
| "un control cada 3 a 6 meses" (section 1) | PSCV: every 3 months at high CV risk, 6 months at moderate, 6-12 months at low | **Substantiated, and conservative.** See 3.3. |

The fourth row matters as much as the first three. The PRD's core premise — that the interval
between controls is longer than the disease tolerates — is the one thing here that checks out, and
for low-risk patients the real interval is longer than the PRD claims. **The argument survives; the
statistics attached to it do not.**

Proposed replacement wording is in `docs/research/README.md` rather than here, since editing the PRD
is Felipe's call.

## 1. Waiting lists, with provenance

### 1.1 Where the numbers come from

The authoritative source is the quarterly **Glosa 06** report that MINSAL is legally obliged to send
to Congress. Two editions were consulted:

- **Q1 2026**, cut-off 31 March 2026, published July 2026. Legal basis Glosa 06 of Ley N°21.796.
  This is the most recent edition located and the one that should be cited at the Lab.
- **Q3 2025**, cut-off 30 September 2025, extraction 14 October 2025. Legal basis Ley de Presupuesto
  N°21.722. Consulted for the trend and for the GES delay tables.

Methodology, stated in the reports themselves: figures come from **SIGTE** (Sistema de Gestión de
Tiempos de Espera), cover the 29 Servicios de Salud, and count **non-GES** waiting lists separately
from delayed GES guarantees. The reports give both a mean and a median for every cut, and they carry
an explicit warning that people and records are not the same unit:

> El total de personas no es la suma de prestaciones, pues una misma persona puede tener varias
> derivaciones. El total de interconsultas sí es sumable.

That distinction is the source of most of the confusion in press coverage, and of the PRD's first
figure.

### 1.2 The national figures, Q1 2026

At 31 March 2026:

| | Records | People | Records per person | Mean days | Median days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Consultas Nuevas de Especialidad (CNE) | 2.513.203 | 2.088.245 | 1,20 | **329** | **236** |
| Intervenciones Quirúrgicas (IQ) | 458.109 | 398.496 | 1,15 | **383** | **259** |

On "2,4 millones de personas": no published figure equals it. The candidates are 2.088.245 people
(CNE), 2.513.203 records (CNE), or 2.486.741 if you add CNE and IQ people — which the report's own
warning tells you not to do, because a person on both lists is counted twice. The honest statements
available are "más de 2 millones de personas esperan una primera consulta de especialidad" or "más
de 2,5 millones de interconsultas pendientes", and they are different claims about different units.

On the day counts: the PRD says *promedio*, so the mean is the right comparison, and the mean is 329
for consultations and 383 for surgery. Neither passes 400 or 500. The figures are not absurd — they
are roughly what the situation looked like earlier — but they are wrong now, and stated as current.

### 1.3 Where 400 and 500 could have come from

Not established, but three plausible origins, offered so the claim can be traced rather than
guessed at:

- **Regional rather than national.** Individual Servicios de Salud do exceed those thresholds. At 30
  September 2025 the CNE mean was 470 days in Tarapacá, 445 in Antofagasta and 409 in Arauco; the
  surgical mean was 520 days in O'Higgins and 482 in Chiloé. A statement like "over 500 days for
  surgery" is true of exactly one Servicio de Salud, not of Chile.
- **Older data.** The Q3 2025 report notes the surgical median peaked at **661 days in 2021** during
  the pandemic. Figures from that period are much higher and still circulate.
- **A different unit or a think-tank cut.** Not investigated.

**Unverified:** which of these the Lab's framing actually used. What would verify it: asking the Lab
organisers for the source of their figure. Worth doing before the pitch, since if the Lab briefing
document itself carries the 2.4M figure, contradicting it on stage needs to be deliberate.

### 1.4 The trend runs against the PRD's rhetorical framing

Both reports document sustained improvement, and a judge from the health administration will know
this. Q3 2025: the CNE median fell 22 days in one quarter (264 to 242), the surgical median fell 33
days (297 to 264, the lowest since December 2019), 2.088.183 people left the waiting lists during
2025, and discharge capacity grew 35,44% in nine months. Q1 2026 continues it: CNE median 236, IQ
median 259.

This does not weaken PreventIA's argument, and the pitch should not pretend the numbers are getting
worse. **The argument that survives is about the interval between controls, not the queue length.**
A patient can be perfectly served by the GES system, seen on time, and still be alone for three to
six months. That gap is structural and is not addressed by shortening any queue.

### 1.5 Relevance to older adults

At 30 September 2025, people aged 65 and over accounted for **29,7%** of surgical waiting-list
interconsultas, with the 15-64 group at 59,7%. The comparable breakdown for CNE was not extracted.

## 2. GES: what is guaranteed, and what is not

### 2.1 The regime

GES operates under **Ley 19.966**. **Decreto GES N°72**, in force since October 2022, expanded the
regime to **87 health problems and 318 garantías de oportunidad**, 18% more than the previous decree
(MINSAL Glosa 06, Q3 2025). The Superintendencia de Salud currently lists **90** health problems
(consulted 3 August 2026).

**Unverified:** which decree raised the count from 87 to 90, and when. What would verify it: the
decree history on BCN Ley Chile, or the Superintendencia's own normative page.

### 2.2 The guarantees for our two covered conditions

From the Superintendencia de Salud, the regulator, consulted 3 August 2026.

**Hipertensión arterial primaria o esencial en personas de 15 años y más**

| Guarantee | Content |
|---|---|
| Acceso | Persons 15+ with persistently elevated blood pressure, ≥140/90 mm Hg |
| Oportunidad — diagnostic confirmation | 45 days from suspicion |
| Oportunidad — treatment start | 24 hours from diagnostic confirmation |
| Oportunidad — specialist care | 45 days from medical indication |
| Protección financiera | 0% FONASA A-D; 20% isapre |

**Diabetes mellitus tipo 2**

| Guarantee | Content |
|---|---|
| Acceso | Any age, with suspicion or diagnosis, from the physician's suspicion |
| Oportunidad — diagnostic confirmation | 30 days from first consultation |
| Oportunidad — treatment start | 24 hours from diagnostic confirmation |
| Oportunidad — specialist care | 60 days from referral, per medical indication |
| Oportunidad — periodic control | Annual fundus examination, or within 90 days of indication |
| Protección financiera | 0% FONASA A-D; 20% isapre |

### 2.3 The finding that matters most for the product

**Every GES oportunidad guarantee above is about getting into care. None of them guarantees anything
about the interval between controls once a patient is in it.**

The single exception in either condition is the annual fundus examination in diabetes, which is a
screening test for a complication, not a follow-up contact.

This is the precise legal shape of the gap PreventIA claims to fill, and stating it this way is far
stronger than a waiting-list statistic. A hypertensive patient is guaranteed confirmation in 45 days
and treatment within 24 hours of that, and then, as a matter of law, nothing at all until they
happen to be seen again. The guarantees are real, they are being met at a rate above 97%, and the
gap exists anyway. That is a structural argument, not a complaint about underperformance, and it is
much harder for a management-criteria judge to dismiss.

### 2.4 Heart failure is not a GES problem at all

Verified against the Superintendencia de Salud's list of health problems (consulted 3 August 2026).
The cardiac problems in the regime are **cardiopatías congénitas operables** (GES Nº2), **infarto al
corazón** (GES Nº5), and **trastornos de generación de impulsos y conducción que requieren
marcapaso** (GES Nº25). Heart failure appears nowhere. It also appears nowhere in the GES tables of
either Glosa 06 report.

Note for accuracy: commercial sources aimed at isapre customers state that heart failure is GES-
covered. They are wrong, or are loosely describing cover for a related problem. The regulator's list
is the authority and it does not include it. This is worth knowing precisely because someone on the
panel may repeat the commercial claim.

Consequences for PreventIA, and they cut both ways:

- **The follow-up gap is widest exactly where the disease is most dangerous.** Heart failure has no
  legal maximum waiting time, no guaranteed treatment start, and no guaranteed control. It is also
  the condition with the richest set of self-reportable warning signs (workstream 1) and the one
  where the MINSAL guide itself sets a patient contact threshold.
- **It is not a PSCV target condition either.** The PSCV covers hypertension, diabetes and
  dyslipidaemia as cardiovascular risk factors. **Unverified:** what programme, if any, structures
  heart failure follow-up in Chilean primary care. What would verify it: asking a PSCV or cardiology
  team directly. This is a real hole in my understanding and I am not going to fill it by inference.

### 2.5 GES compliance and where it fails

At 30 September 2025: over **97%** of a universe of **3,7 million** oportunidad guarantees were met.
For those that were delayed, the mean delay was 136 days and the median 71 days — the report notes
that most delays are short and a small tail inflates the mean.

Delayed guarantees by health problem, both cuts:

| Health problem | People with delayed guarantees, 30 Sep 2025 | 31 Mar 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Diabetes mellitus tipo 2 | **12.952 — the single largest of any health problem** | 6.850 (9,04%, second largest) |
| Hipertensión arterial primaria o esencial | 456 (mean delay 54,7 days, median 25) | 441 |

Diabetes being the largest single source of delayed GES guarantees in the country, and roughly
halving in two quarters, is a fact worth having in the pitch. It says the system is under real strain
on precisely one of our target conditions, and that it is actively working on it.

The contrast between the two conditions is also informative: hypertension generates almost no delayed
guarantees. Its guarantees are cheap to meet — a blood pressure measurement and a prescription — so
compliance is high while the between-controls gap is untouched. High GES compliance and a real
follow-up gap coexist comfortably, which is the argument in 2.3 restated with numbers.

## 3. The Programa de Salud Cardiovascular

Source: MINSAL, *Orientación Técnica Programa de Salud Cardiovascular*, 2017.

**Currency caveat:** this 2017 edition is the most recent Orientación Técnica located. A third-party
site indexes an "OT PSCV" covering 2020-2026, which was not verified against a MINSAL-hosted copy.
**Unverified.** What would verify it: the MINSAL or redcronicas.minsal.cl document listing.

### 3.1 What it is

Created in **2002** by merging the separate hypertension and diabetes programmes into one primary-care
programme organised around **global cardiovascular risk** rather than individual risk factors. It
covers people **15 and over**. Since 2009 risk has been estimated with Framingham tables adapted to
the Chilean population, updated by a 2014 consensus.

### 3.2 Two phases

**Compensation phase**, until targets are met:

- **Hypertension:** control **every 2 weeks, maximum 4**, to adjust drugs and assess adverse effects,
  aiming to reach target within 3 months.
- **Diabetes:** during dose adjustment, capillary glucose every 2-3 days at the centre or at home;
  HbA1c 3 months after adjustment ends.
- **Dyslipidaemia:** lipid profile 6-8 weeks after starting or changing treatment.

**Follow-up phase**, once compensated. Verbatim:

> Frecuencia sugerida:
> - RCV alto cada 3 meses.
> - RCV moderado cada 6 meses.
> - RCV bajo cada 6-12 meses.

This is the direct source for the PRD's "un control cada 3 a 6 meses", and it substantiates it for
high and moderate risk. For low risk the real interval is 6 to 12 months, so the PRD understates its
own case.

### 3.3 Targets are already age-adjusted, and the programme already knows about frailty

Relevant because PreventIA's population is exactly the one the programme treats differently.

- Blood pressure target <140/90 mm Hg, but in people **80 and over** it is **<150/90, and >120/60** —
  a floor as well as a ceiling.
- HbA1c targets for people **65 and over** are stratified by clinical state: <7-7,5% if healthy and
  independent, <8% if frail, <8,5% in very complex health states, with the note that in end-of-life
  care the goal is only to avoid symptomatic hyperglycaemia.
- Frailty for this purpose is defined as at least one of: over 75, significant chronic comorbidity,
  malnutrition (BMI <23), dependence in basic activities of daily living (Barthel ≤60), life
  expectancy under 5 years, frequent falls, severe depression, moderate-to-severe cognitive
  impairment, high social and economic risk.

Two implications. First, the synthetic cohort should be built against these definitions rather than
invented, and the frailty criteria are a better cohort specification than anything we would write
ourselves. Second, **"compensated" is not one number** — an HbA1c that is on target for a frail
85-year-old is off target for a healthy 66-year-old. Any rule table that keys on absolute values
without the patient's frailty state will be wrong for someone. **REQUIRES CLINICAL REVIEW.**

### 3.4 Who actually runs follow-up

From Flujograma 1 and the role descriptions: after the physician's admission consultation, routine
controls are run by **enfermera universitaria and nutricionista**, whose listed tasks include
"Evaluación y refuerzo de la adherencia" and "Notificación de efectos adversos". The patient goes to
a **medical** consultation when risk factors are off target, and to **interconsulta** after repeated
consultations without reaching targets.

**Follow-up in the PSCV is nurse-led.** That is a direct match for the escalation surface in ADR-0006
and for the PRD's "matrona o médico de cabecera", and it should be said plainly in the pitch: the
person PreventIA hands a case to already exists, already owns adherence, and already owns adverse-
effect reporting. PreventIA is not proposing a new role.

### 3.5 Two instruments the programme already mandates

Both matter more to workstream 3 than to this one, but they are sourced here.

- **Adherence: Test de Morisky Green Levine-4**, named in the OT (Anexo 11) and recommended
  explicitly for the compensation phase: "En el seguimiento de los pacientes, siempre se debe evaluar
  y fomentar la adherencia a la terapia. En la fase inicial de compensación este aspecto se vuelve
  crucial, para esto se recomienda utilizar el Test de Morisky Green Levine – 4".
- **Depression: PHQ-9**, suggested as screening for people with diabetes or prior cardiovascular
  disease, described as "validada en Chile, en personas de 20 años y más, con una sensibilidad de
  92% y una especificidad de 89%" (Anexo 10).

The adherence one is significant for the product: **PreventIA does not need to invent an adherence
measure.** The programme already has one, the clinical team already knows it, and aligning to it is
both cheaper and more credible than a bespoke score. Whether a conversational agent can administer
or approximate it is workstream 3's question.

The depression one bears on the open question from workstream 1 about whether depression is in scope.
The programme screens for it in exactly our population, which is an argument that noticing it is not
scope creep. It remains **REQUIRES CLINICAL REVIEW**.

### 3.6 Telemedicine is already in the flowchart

Flujograma 1 lists interconsulta in two modalities, "Telemedicina" and "Tradicional". Remote contact
is not a foreign concept being introduced to the programme; it is an existing referral route. Useful
for the adoption-pathway question, and honest: it is a specialist referral modality, not routine
between-controls contact.

### 3.7 What happens between controls today

The direct answer to the brief's question, and it is a partial one.

What the PSCV documents describe: scheduled controls at the frequencies in 3.2, five multidisciplinary
self-management workshops ("Taller PSCV: Automanejo, 5 talleres multidisciplinarios") in Flujograma 1,
family counselling of 2-3 sessions on reaching targets, and telemedicine as an interconsulta
modality.

What I did not find in any document consulted: **any routine, scheduled contact between controls.**
The programme's structure is a sequence of appointments with education attached, not a continuous
follow-up.

I am not going to state that as "nothing happens between controls", because absence from a national
technical guidance document is not evidence that individual centres do nothing — many run their own
telephone follow-up, and the OT would not necessarily record it. **Unverified.** What would verify
it: asking a working PSCV team at a CESFAM what they actually do between controls, which is a
question for the clinical teammate and takes one conversation.

This matters for the pitch's honesty. "Nadie está mirando" is defensible as a statement about what
the system guarantees. It is not verified as a statement about what every centre does.
